# nuée — ingredient & claim sources

nuée is a fictional brand and this serum does not exist. Its ingredient list is a typical
"hydrating + brightening" facial serum, and every claim on the page is kept to what the
published research below supports.

| On the page | What the research says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyaluronic Acid** — "Holds water in skin"; **Deep Hydration** row | Hyaluronic acid is a key skin molecule with a very high capacity to bind and retain water; its loss is linked to skin ageing and dryness. | Papakonstantinou E., Roth M., Karakiulakis G. *Hyaluronic acid: A key molecule in skin aging.* Dermato-Endocrinology 2012; 4(3): 253–258. https://doi.org/10.4161/derm.21923 |
| **Niacinamide** — "Evens skin tone"; **Brighter, Even Tone** row | In a 12-week split-face trial, 5% topical niacinamide reduced hyperpigmented spots, redness, fine lines and sallowness, and improved elasticity. | Bissett D.L., Oblong J.E., Berge C.A. *Niacinamide: A B vitamin that improves aging facial skin appearance.* Dermatologic Surgery 2005; 31(7 Pt 2): 860–865. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1524-4725.2005.31732 |
| **Vitamin C** — "Brightens & protects"; **Brighter, Even Tone** row | Vitamin C is an antioxidant that supports collagen formation and inhibits melanin production, which is why it is used against dullness and pigmentation. | Pullar J.M., Carr A.C., Vissers M.C.M. *The Roles of Vitamin C in Skin Health.* Nutrients 2017; 9(8): 866. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9080866 |
| **Squalane** — "Weightless moisture"; **Light as Mist** row | Squalene is a natural component of human sebum; squalane, its stable hydrogenated form, is used as a light, non-greasy emollient that spreads and absorbs easily. | Kim S.-K., Karadeniz F. *Biological importance and applications of squalene and squalane.* Advances in Food and Nutrition Research 2012; 65: 223–233. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-416003-3.00014-7 |
| **Vitamin E** — "Antioxidant care" | Vitamin E (tocopherol) is the skin's main lipid-soluble antioxidant and is widely used topically; combined with vitamin C it gives greater protection against UV-induced damage than either alone. | Thiele J.J., Ekanayake-Mudiyanselage S. *Vitamin E in human skin: organ-specific physiology and considerations for its use in dermatology.* Molecular Aspects of Medicine 2007; 28(5–6): 646–667. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mam.2007.06.001 · Lin J.-Y. et al. *UV photoprotection by combination topical antioxidants vitamin C and vitamin E.* J Am Acad Dermatol 2003; 48(6): 866–874. https://doi.org/10.1067/mjd.2003.425 |
